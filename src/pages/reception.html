🎬 A palace with screen credits

  • Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 2 — features the estate's grounds and the famous Elephant Oak

Bishop Morton builds in brick (1485)

The Old Palace was built around 1485 by John Morton, Bishop of Ely and later Archbishop of Canterbury. His grand brick palace, laid the year the Wars of the Roses ended, is among the finest surviving medieval brick architecture in England. When Henry VIII helped himself to church property in the Dissolution, he helped himself to Hatfield too — and used it as a royal nursery, parking all three of his children there at various points: Mary, Edward, and a red-headed girl called Elizabeth.

Elizabeth under the oak (1533–1558)

It's Elizabeth who haunts the place, in the best way. She spent much of her childhood and her politically perilous twenties at Hatfield, reading, translating, and outwitting people who wanted her head. In November 1558, so the story goes, she was in the park (under an oak tree, tradition insists) when riders arrived to tell her that her sister was dead and she was Queen of England. She is said to have dropped to the grass and quoted the Psalms: "This is the Lord's doing; it is marvellous in our eyes." Days later she held her very first Council of State in the banqueting hall of the Old Palace — the same hall where, on 29 May, you'll be holding a glass and (we hope) dancing with abandon.

Robert Cecil's demolition-grade confidence (1607 → your dance floor)

In 1607 the palace passed to Robert Cecil, Elizabeth's spymaster's son. He swapped houses with King James I, built the vast Jacobean Hatfield House next door, and — with the confidence of a man who owned too many palaces — demolished three of the Old Palace's four wings to reuse the bricks. The banqueting hall alone survived, which turns out to be the wing we needed. The gardens around it were first laid out by John Tradescant the Elder, the great plant-hunter, who stocked them with trees and flowers never before grown in England. Five centuries of history, one dance floor.
