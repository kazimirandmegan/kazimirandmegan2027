Europe

Your 20-minute train terminates at St Pancras International — the Eurostar terminal. The continent starts at the end of our branch line.

Paris

Eurostar · 2h16 · from ~£80 return

Croissants for breakfast, home for a very late dinner. Book the Louvre; the Marais needs nothing but appetite.

Amsterdam

Eurostar direct · ~4h · from ~£90 return

Canals, bikes, Van Gogh (book weeks ahead), pancakes the size of bin lids.

Brussels & Bruges

Eurostar 2h + 1h local · from ~£80 return

Beer with monastic seriousness, chocolate with none. Bruges is a medieval film set.

Lille

Eurostar · 1h22 · from ~£70 return

France's best-kept day-trip secret: gorgeous old town, serious art, legendary bakeries.

Edinburgh

Train · 4h30 · fly ~£50–100 return

A castle on a volcano, closes and cobbles. Arthur's Seat at sunrise, legs permitting.

Dublin

Fly from Luton · ~1h · ~£40–90 return

The black stuff at the source, the Howth cliff walk, music until they turn the lights on.

Copenhagen

Fly · ~2h · ~£50–110 return

Design, harbour swims, Tivoli by night, pastries that ruin all other pastries.

Barcelona

Fly · ~2h15 · ~£50–120 return

Gaudí in the morning, tapas at ten, a beach in between.

Fares are rough guides for a budget-airline (Ryanair, easyJet, Vueling, Wizz) return from a London airport around early June — think Luton, Stansted, Gatwick, all reachable from St Albans. Book two to three months ahead and travel light (cabin bag only) for the low end; add roughly £30–50 for a checked bag or a flexible ticket. Eurostar prices are cheapest booked early too. Always check live prices before you commit.

Practicalities

  • Book Eurostar 2–3 months out for the good fares; check entry requirements for your passport
  • Left-luggage at St Pancras lets you day-trip straight off your suitcase
  • Cards work everywhere on the continent — no currency wads required
