Choose your adventure

For sightseeing
  • Windsor — Historic riverside town centred on the world's oldest occupied castle. Tour Windsor Castle and St George's Chapel, walk through the old town, and cross the bridge to Eton. ~1 hr 30 min · £20–35
  • Canterbury — Compact medieval cathedral city and UNESCO World Heritage site. Visit Canterbury Cathedral, explore the cobbled centre, and walk beside the River Stour and old city walls. ~1 hr 35 min · £35–55
  • Stonehenge & Salisbury — Britain's most famous prehistoric monument paired with a magnificent medieval cathedral city. Visit Stonehenge, then Salisbury Cathedral and its surviving copy of Magna Carta. ~2 hr 45 min · £55–85
  • Seven Sisters & Eastbourne — Dramatic white chalk cliffs above the English Channel. Walk the coastal path, visit Beachy Head, and finish with fish and chips by the sea. Stay well back from cliff edges. ~2 hr 30 min · £40–65
For culture
  • Cambridge — Beautiful university city of ancient colleges, chapels, and riverside lawns. Tour a college, see King's College Chapel, go punting on the Cam, and visit the Fitzwilliam Museum. ~1 hr 20–40 min · £25–40
  • Oxford — Ancient university city of colleges, libraries, and museums. Visit the Bodleian Library, climb a tower, and explore the Ashmolean Museum. ~1 hr 45–2 hr · £30–50
  • Bath — Elegant Georgian city built around Britain's best-preserved Roman baths. Visit the Roman Baths and Bath Abbey, admire the Royal Crescent, and cross Pulteney Bridge. ~2 hr 25 min · £45–90
  • Winchester — Handsome cathedral city and former capital of Anglo-Saxon England. Visit Winchester Cathedral, see the Great Hall's Round Table, and walk through the water meadows. ~1 hr 55 min · £30–55
  • Stratford-upon-Avon — Shakespeare's hometown of Tudor buildings and riverside scenery. See Shakespeare's Birthplace, Anne Hathaway's Cottage, and catch an RSC performance if timings allow. ~2 hr 40 min · £40–75
  • York — Remarkably preserved city with Roman, Viking, and medieval history. Visit York Minster, walk the city walls, explore the Shambles, and choose between the Railway Museum or JORVIK Viking Centre. ~2 hr 25–40 min · £50–120
For kids
  • Harry Potter Studio Tour — Original sets, costumes, and filmmaking secrets from the films. Explore the Great Hall, Diagon Alley, and Forbidden Forest; sample butterbeer. Book well ahead — it sells out. ~15–25 min by car · ~£30–70 return taxi; admission extra
  • Warwick Castle — Large medieval castle with battlements, towers, and live historical displays. Explore the State Rooms and castle walls, then watch seasonal jousting, falconry, or archery. ~2 hr 15–30 min · £35–70
  • Hampton Court Palace — Henry VIII's enormous Tudor palace beside the Thames. Explore the kitchens and State Apartments, get lost in the famous maze, and wander the formal gardens. ~1 hr 30 min · £15–25
For foodies
  • Whitstable — Small Kent seaside town famous for oysters and colourful beach huts. Eat oysters by the harbour, browse Harbour Market, and walk the coast to Tankerton. ~1 hr 55 min · £35–55
  • The Cotswolds — Rolling countryside of honey-coloured villages, farm shops, and country pubs. Build a driving route through villages like Stow-on-the-Wold or Broadway, with a pub lunch and farm-shop stop. ~1 hr 45 min–2 hr 30 min by car · ~£40–70 per car
For a date day
  • Rye — A beautifully preserved hilltop town with cobbled streets, crooked houses, and atmospheric old inns. Wander Mermaid Street, browse antique shops, visit Rye Castle, and settle in for a long lunch or early dinner. ~1 hr 50 min · £35–60
For a rainy day
  • Brighton — Lively seaside city with the eccentric Royal Pavilion, independent shops, and a famous pier. Visit the Pavilion, explore the Lanes, play in the pier arcades, and have fish and chips between showers. The Thameslink runs direct from St Albans. ~1 hr 40 min · £25–45
