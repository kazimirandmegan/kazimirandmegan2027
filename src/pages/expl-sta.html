St Albans

Two thousand years of history, one very good market, and the finest pub-per-mile ratio in the Home Counties

St Albans is a historic cathedral city in Hertfordshire, England, located 20 miles north of London. It is famous for its rich Roman heritage (originally the ancient city of Verulamium), stunning medieval architecture and vibrant market culture. St Albans takes its name from the first British saint, Alban.

Choose your adventure

Must-see
  • St Albans Cathedral — Free entry. One of England's oldest and most beautiful medieval cathedrals — you'll be here on Saturday, so get a preview. Don't miss the medieval wall paintings and the tower tours.
  • Verulamium Museum & Park — Roman Britain at its most vivid. The park has a genuine mosaic and hypocaust in situ, plus a lake, a playground, and a splash park in summer. The museum is wonderful.
  • The Clock Tower — England's only surviving medieval town belfry, right in the city centre. Climb it for the view and the history; it costs almost nothing and is genuinely spectacular.
  • The Street Market (Wed & Sat) — Trading since 1553, older than the reception venue. Fresh produce, street food, local makers — one of the best in Hertfordshire.
For eating & drinking

We've been sampling restaurants around St Albans in part because we love food, but also so we could put a solid recommendations list here for you. Enjoy the fruits of our labour:

Breakfast

  • Toast — An incredible brunch spot. Americans: try bubble and squeak here — a classic British breakfast, and Mother of the Bride-approved.
  • The Waffle House St Albans — Near where many of you are staying at St Michael's. This is the polar opposite of the American Waffle House — really yummy and a beautiful sit-down place for brunch.
  • Hatch and Street Cafe — Also solid breakfast options. The Breakfast Club is great and right near our house.
  • Proto Artisan Bakery — Town's best pastries (our humble opinion).
  • Silver Palate St Albans — Very centrally located town classic (though a bit pricey).

Coffee

  • Megan has sampled all the local cafés and safely recommends: Brad & Dills, Nkora, Charlie's Coffee, and Baruccio — in addition to the coffee at all of the breakfast spots above.

Lunch & dinner

  • La Cosa Nostra — One of our favourite restaurants in St Albans. A five-minute walk from the Uzwyshyn-Jones residence, well-priced, and super authentic Italian.
  • Oasis — Our other favourite. The Moroccan food is so good and the portions/value for money is insane. Super easy as a takeaway too.
  • Per Tutti — A beautiful Italian restaurant near the Cathedral.
  • Won — Recently opened; great Korean food (though a bit pricey).
  • Sukawatee — A nice local Thai place in the city centre. Thai Square is another good option, very central and in a historic building.
  • For British food: local pubs like Hare & Hounds (also Constance's favourite pub) and the White Lion.
  • For slightly nicer options: Mediterranean food at Tabure, or elevated pub fare at Dylan's at The Kings Arms.

Drinks & sweets

  • Darlish — Potentially the best ice cream you'll ever have. Locally made and on the walk from the city centre to our house. Keep an eye out for opening times — it closes annoyingly early.
  • Mad Squirrel Tap — A local craft brewery worth trying, as is Beer Shop, which has a lot of Belgian beers.
  • No Nuisance Bar — Named after the iconic sign on the St Albans Clock Tower. A cool place on George Street, right by the Cathedral and gorgeous at night.
  • Bishop's Cave and MUST — Nice wine bars with cosy interiors.
For history buffs
  • The Roman Mosaic and Hypocaust — Exquisitely preserved Roman mosaic housed in a dynamic and exciting modern building in Verulamium Park, just a short walk from Verulamium Museum.
  • Roman Theatre of Verulamium — The only visible example of its kind in Britain, and still used today. It was linked to temples dedicated to Romano-British gods and was used for religious festivals, armed combat, and wild beast shows.
  • de Havilland Aircraft Museum — Britain's first aviation collection. The core of the collection is the unique Mosquito prototype — the only original WW2 prototype in the world.
  • St Albans Cathedral — Free entry, tower tours, medieval wall paintings; you'll be back Saturday anyway.
  • The Clock Tower — England's only surviving medieval town belfry; climb it for pennies.
For families
  • Verulamium Park — Playground, summer splash park, lake, and ducks of distinction.
  • Warner Bros. Studio Tour London — The Making of Harry Potter — Much of Harry Potter was filmed nearby at Warner Bros. Studio. See the iconic sets of The Great Hall, The Forbidden Forest, Platform 9¾ and Diagon Alley. An experience of a lifetime — book well ahead.
  • Willows Activity Farm — Engineered to exhaust the under-tens.
  • The Cathedral café + lawns — Step-free everywhere, toddler-tolerant everywhere.
For pub scholars
  • Ye Olde Fighting Cocks — Claimant to England's oldest pub; conduct your own peer review. Right next to the Cathedral and a short walk from Verulamium Park.
  • The Horn, The Jolly Sailor, The White Swan — The after-party shortlist, available for reconnaissance.
  • Hare & Hounds — Constance's favourite pub, and easy to see why.
  • No Nuisance Bar — Named after the famous Clock Tower sign; great atmosphere at night.

For walkers
  • River Ver strolls — Walk straight from the city centre along the River Ver; peaceful and surprisingly countryside-feeling for a city walk.
  • Gorhambury Estate — The Roman, Elizabethan and 18th-century history of this area is encapsulated in the Roman Theatre, Old Gorhambury and Gorhambury House. Countryside miles past Francis Bacon's ruined manor.
  • The Alban Way — Runs along the former route of the Hatfield to St Albans branch line of the Great Northern Railway. Opened in 1865, it was used until 1969 and is now a lovely traffic-free walk.
  • St Albans Pilgrim Trails — Explore the city's ancient churches and discover their stories on this self-guided trail.
  • Country walks in Hertfordshire — St Albans' wider district has ample walking routes through the beautiful Hertfordshire countryside, enjoyable all year round.
For a lazy morning
  • The market (Wed & Sat) — Trading since 1553; coffee since slightly later. One of the best street markets in Hertfordshire.
  • Books On The Hill — If you ask Megan what she loves about St Albans, inevitably Books On The Hill will lead her list as the best bookshop she's ever found (there are also beautiful historical reading rooms connected). You can spend hours relaxing there.
