Travel Essentials

Do I need a visa?
  • Entry: US citizens (and most other non-European visitors) need a UK Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA).
  • Apply through the official government website before travel. Avoid unofficial sites that charge extra.
  • Cost: £20.
Will my phone work in the UK?
  • Check with your mobile provider before travelling, as international roaming charges can be expensive on some US plans.
  • We recommend getting an eSIM. Consider Airalo or Saily — just download their app and go from there.
What plugs do I need?
  • The UK uses the three-pin Type G plug (230V). Bring a travel adapter.
  • Many hotels also provide USB-A and USB-C charging ports.
  • Fun fact: British plugs are objectively excellent engineering. For evidence, see Tom Scott's explanation.
How do I download WhatsApp?
  • WhatsApp is a free messaging app for iPhone and Android. Download it here before you travel.
  • It works over Wi-Fi and mobile data, helping you avoid roaming charges.
  • We'll use a group chat to share updates, timings, and meeting points throughout the wedding week, so we strongly recommend installing it.
Do I need a hire car?
  • For St Albans and London, a rental car is usually more hassle than it's worth. Walking, taxis, trains, and the Tube will cover most journeys. Check your route on Google Maps before ordering a taxi — often public transport is faster.
  • If you do want a rental car, airport pickup is usually easiest. Major providers include Enterprise and Sixt.
  • For a local option, see St Albans Van & Car Hire.
  • Remember: we drive on the left and roundabouts are everywhere. If you're feeling adventurous, look up the Magic Roundabout.
What do I do in a medical emergency?
  • 999 — Emergency services (ambulance, fire, police). Use for life-threatening emergencies.
  • 111 — NHS non-emergency medical helpline. Call or visit NHS 111 for urgent but non-life-threatening concerns.
  • Pharmacies — Boots is the UK's largest pharmacy chain, with several branches in St Albans. Pharmacies can help with minor illnesses, prescriptions, and over-the-counter medicines.
What is British weather really like?
  • Statistically, expect highs of 17–20°C (63–68°F), lows of 9–11°C (48–52°F), long evenings (sunset after 9pm), and roughly a one-in-three chance of rain on any given day.
  • In practice, late May can deliver sunshine, heavy rain, and a brisk wind before lunch.
  • Pack:
    • One warm layer
    • A compact umbrella
    • Sunglasses (optimism)
    • Shoes that can handle damp grass
  • Air conditioning is not standard in many UK buildings, so check with your hotel if this is important to you.

Food, Drink & Local Customs

Is tap water safe to drink?
  • Yes. Tap water is safe to drink and is available free in restaurants, pubs, and hotels.
  • You can ask for tap water anywhere, although it may not come with ice.
Can I drink alcohol in public?
  • Drinking alcohol is prohibited in some places, particularly on certain forms of public transport.
  • Elsewhere, it's generally permitted and enjoyed. A picnic with a tin of Pimm's is a classic British experience.
Do I really have to queue?

Yes. Queueing is Britain's national pastime.

How do I pay for things?
  • Cards: Contactless payment is accepted pretty much everywhere, including trains and buses. Visa and Mastercard are more widely accepted than Amex. Most places no longer accept chip payments, so make sure you have a card that you can tap and pay with.
  • Cash: You are unlikely to need cash. Most businesses accept card payments.
Do I need to tip?
  • Tipping is appreciated but less common than in the US.
  • Many restaurants add a 10–12.5% service charge automatically, particularly for groups of six or more. Check your bill before tipping — if there is already a service charge, there's no need to add more.
  • Tipping is not expected when ordering drinks at a pub bar.
