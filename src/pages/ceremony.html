St Albans Cathedral: Seventeen centuries, four clicks

🎬 As seen on screen

A saint, a swapped cloak, and this very hill (c. 300 AD)

Around the year 300 (nobody can quite agree — it was a while ago), a Roman citizen of Verulamium named Alban sheltered a Christian priest fleeing persecution. Moved by his guest's faith, Alban converted, swapped cloaks with the priest, and handed himself over in his place. He was marched up the hill outside town and executed — becoming Britain's first saint — and the hilltop where he died is the very spot where you'll be sitting. People have worshipped there continuously ever since, which makes it the oldest site of continuous Christian worship in Britain. As wedding venues go, the reviews run long.

Roman bricks

The Shrine of St Alban was the reason for the Abbey's foundation and the town that grew up around it, and it is said that King Offa of Mercia founded a monastery here in 793. The building you'll see, though, is substantially Norman: after 1066, Abbot Paul of Caen rebuilt it at colossal scale, and — in history's finest example of recycling — his masons robbed the bricks from the abandoned Roman city of Verulamium at the bottom of the hill.

The town of St Albans itself has a rich history, dating back to Roman times as Verulamium, and has played a significant role in English events, including the signing of Magna Carta and battles during the Wars of the Roses.

Pilgrims, England's longest nave and the birthplace of hot cross buns

For centuries this was one of the great pilgrimage churches of Europe. Pilgrims queued at the shrine of St Alban. The nave they walked up is still the longest of any cathedral in England — at 85 metres, the walk down the aisle is genuinely one of the longest legally available.

St Albans Cathedral is also the birthplace of the hot cross bun. In 1361, a monk named Brother Thomas Rocliffe created the original "Alban Bun" to distribute to the local poor on Good Friday. Today, the cathedral keeps this nearly 700-year-old tradition alive by baking and selling the historic precursor during Lent.

The Cathedral today

Today, St Albans Cathedral operates as a vibrant hub of continuous Christian worship, community life, and heritage. Every June, the Cathedral leads the town in Alban Day celebrations, a festival that retells the story of Alban's martyrdom, complete with 12-foot puppets (ask Kazimir, Maksym or Zoryana what their roles in this have been over the years).

St Albans School, right next door, is one of the oldest in the world. Former students include Nicholas Breakspear, a cleric who went on to become Pope Adrian IV, and physicist Stephen Hawking.
