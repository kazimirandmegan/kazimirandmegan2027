Getting to London

You are closer than you think. From St Albans City station, fast Thameslink trains reach St Pancras International in about 20 minutes, running several times an hour from early morning to late at night — the same line carries on through the City (Farringdon, Blackfriars, London Bridge) and down to Gatwick and Brighton, so you often step off right where you want to be. No advance ticket needed: just tap in and out with any contactless card or phone and the daily fare caps itself automatically.

Alternatively, St Albans Abbey station runs a little branch line to Watford, and Luton Airport is about 15 minutes up the road — handy for the budget flights on the Europe page.

Getting around London

Once you're in town, one contactless card does everything — Tube, bus, DLR, Overground, tram and most river boats — and the same daily cap applies across all of them. You never need a paper ticket or an Oyster card as a visitor. A few official maps worth saving to your phone before you go:

  • Tube map (PDF) — the iconic one; every line and zone
  • Bus maps — area-by-area spider maps; buses are the scenic, cheap way to see the city from the top deck
  • Cycle map — the Cycleways network and Santander Cycles ("Boris bikes") docking stations
  • Walking maps — the "Legible London" wayfinding maps; central London is far more walkable than it looks
  • All TfL maps & the TfL Go app — river services, night tube, step-free access and live journey planning in one place

Rule of thumb: two or three stops apart, walk it — you'll see more and it's often faster than going down and up again. Anything further, the Tube or a bus. Black cabs and Uber both work late into the night.

Choose your adventure

For sightseeing
  • Walk from Westminster to South Bank — See Big Ben, the London Eye, Shakespeare's Globe, the Tate Modern, and Borough Market. Takes roughly 2–3 hours if you're sight-seeing, and is about 2.5 to 3.8 miles depending on your route. One unbeatable afternoon on foot.
  • The big hitters — Tower of London, Buckingham Palace & Changing of the Guard (free, punctual, very shiny), shopping in Covent Garden and Oxford Circus (including the grand London department stores: Harrods, Selfridges, Fortnum & Mason).
  • Visit London's beautiful parks — Hyde Park (go paddle-boarding on the Serpentine Lake), Regent's Park (check out Queen Mary's Rose Garden), Victoria Park, or Hampstead Heath. If you go to Hampstead, go to Parliament Hill viewpoint for city views.
  • Wander Chinatown — Try loads of great food and soak up the atmosphere.
  • Visit London Dungeons — Low-key a highlight for Megan and Kazimir.

If you've been to London before and want to go beyond the big tourist attractions:

  • London's many markets — Central: Exmouth Market (all food, very accessible from St Albans), Seven Dials (food market in Covent Garden), Mercato Metropolitano (a food market in an old church in Oxford Circus), Southbank Centre food market. East: Brick Lane Market, Spitalfields, Columbia Flower Market. West: Portobello Road Market — Megan used to live at the top of it; this is where the movie Notting Hill was filmed. North: Camden Market. South: Brixton Village (right next to where Megan used to work!).
  • Somerset House — A beautiful courtyard with great views overlooking the river. Also houses art exhibitions.
  • St Martin-in-the-Fields — Megan and Kazimir's church in London, known for its world-class musical programme. See a concert here.
  • London's Little Venice — A network of canals through the city. Stop by Word on the Water, a floating bookshop near Kings Cross.
  • Visit Greenwich — In south-east London. A beautiful district on the river, with a great market, the Royal Observatory, the classical buildings of the Old Royal Naval College, and the home of Greenwich Mean Time.
  • Richmond (if you're a Ted Lasso fan) — In southwest London. Aside from being a gorgeous part of the city with a massive park, the show was filmed here.
  • House of Mina Lima — In central London. The graphic design company that created loads of the Harry Potter visuals, which they have on display.
  • Kazimir's East London suggestions — From when he lived there: visit Broadway Market, join a Jack the Ripper tour in Whitechapel, try the famous bagels on Brick Lane.
  • Megan's North London suggestions — From when she lived there: Parkland Walk (a 5km trail) for London greenery and a beautiful view of the city, catch Arsenal if you're a football fan, and the restaurants on Blackstock Road (also in Arsenal).
For culture
  • World-class (and free!) museums — The British Museum, National Gallery, Tate Modern, and the Victoria and Albert (V&A) Museum.
  • A West End show — Buy tickets online (and ahead of time) by searching the show's website, or monitor TodayTix for more last-minute deals.
  • The Royal Ballet or Sadler's Wells — The Royal Ballet for classics; Sadler's Wells for more contemporary. You can get cheap nosebleed seats if you don't want to splurge.
  • Evensong at St Paul's or Westminster Abbey — Free if you go for a service, but has an entry fee otherwise. We would highly recommend seeing at least one of these beautiful cathedrals.
  • Top Secret Comedy Club — In central London; it only costs one pound your first time!
For kids
  • Natural History Museum — free, has dinosaurs, wins automatically
  • London Transport Museum + the front seat on the top deck of any red bus
  • South Bank street performers and the carousel
For foodies

Lunch & dinner

  • Dishoom at any location — incredible Indian food. There's always a queue but it's worth it.
  • The food at Kingly St — including Imad's Syrian Kitchen and La Maritxu Basque Cheesecake shop.
  • Ngon Ngon or What The Pok'k — amazing Vietnamese near Farringdon (direct trains from St Albans).
  • Fish, Wings and Tings in Brixton — Really good Caribbean fare and one of Megan's favourite restaurants.
  • Any of the Big Mamma Italian restaurants.
  • Xi Home Dumplings Bay near Leicester Square or 1+1 rougamo in Holborn for incredible Chinese food.
  • The Cheese Barge in Paddington — Exactly what it sounds like: the most amazing cheese and fresh food on a canal boat.
  • Norfolk Arms — A beautiful pub near Kings Cross that randomly serves amazing Mediterranean tapas.
  • Borough Market — Arrive hungry, leave sideways. A proper Sunday roast at any pub with a fire works too.

Drinks & sweets

  • Coffee — There are amazing coffee shops everywhere, but here are a few central ones Megan recommends: The Fleet Street Press (near the Royal Courts), Kiss the Hippo Coffee (Covent Garden), Project68 (Kings Cross), Chealsons Coffee (near the Tate Modern), and Lever & Bloom Coffee (near Tottenham Court Road).
  • Cahoots Underground — Crazy cocktails in a 1940s London Underground car.
  • Gordon's Wine Bar — A classic Soho wine bar near the Thames, or Voila! Wine Bar near the West End in Covent Garden.
  • In Chinatown, try Kova Patisserie — Megan and her friend Anna's go-to dessert place. Their sea salt cheesecake is the one. Also visit Mamasons Dirty Ice Cream and try the ube bilog (purple ube ice cream in a crepe!).
  • Udderlicious Ice Cream — Amazing in both Covent Garden and Islington.
  • Humble Crumble — A must. Crumble is a very popular British dessert and this is the place to try it.
For history
  • The Churchill War Rooms — Part of the Imperial War Museum, including an underground complex that housed a British government command centre throughout the Second World War.
For date night
  • Sky Garden at golden hour — the best free view in London, book ahead
  • A river walk from St Paul's to the Tower of London, followed by dinner in Shoreditch
  • Jazz at Ronnie Scott's if you're feeling fancy
For a rainy day
  • The museums (see culture — all free, all dry)
  • Leadenhall Market + the Harry Potter filming locations crawl
  • Afternoon tea somewhere warm; you're on holiday
