Getting to and from London

The train

From St Albans City station, Thameslink trains reach London St Pancras International in about 20 minutes, running roughly every 15 minutes from early morning until late at night. The same line carries on through the City (Farringdon, Blackfriars, London Bridge) and down to Gatwick and Brighton — you often step off right where you want to be.

Trains typically depart from platforms 1 and 3 at St Albans City.

Tickets & fares

No advance booking needed. Either buy your ticket at the St Albans station kiosk (look for London St Pancras International) or just tap in and out with any contactless card or phone (Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Apple Pay, Google Pay). Single fares are approx £10–15.

Please note: Oyster Card will not work!

Travelling as a group?

Groups of 3+ off-peak can save by buying Group Save tickets at the station machine. Ask for "Group Save" — you typically get a third off. Off-peak is broadly anything outside 07:00–09:30 and 16:30–19:30 on weekdays. This will be 1/3 cheaper than travelling at peak times.

Key stations on the Thameslink from St Albans

  • St Pancras International — Eurostar terminal, Kings Cross for the tube, pubs and restaurants
  • Farringdon — Elizabeth line, Barbican, Clerkenwell, Exmouth Market
  • City Thameslink / Blackfriars — South Bank, Tate Modern, Borough Market a short walk
  • London Bridge — Borough Market on the doorstep, Shard views, multiple tube lines

St Albans Abbey station (western end of town) runs a branch line to Watford Junction — handy for Watford but not for central London. Use St Albans City for London.

Luton Airport is about 15 minutes up the road — handy for the budget flights on the Europe page.

Getting around London

Once you're in town, one contactless card does everything — Tube, bus, DLR, Overground, tram and most river boats — and the same daily cap applies across all of them. You never need a paper ticket or an Oyster card as a visitor.

As a rule of thumb, if you are two or three stops apart, walk it — you'll see more and it's often faster than going down and up again. Anything further, the Tube or a bus. Black cabs and Uber both work late into the night but can be expensive!

London is a big city of 10 million and will feel busy, hectic and overwhelming. If in doubt, just use Google Maps or Apple Maps for everything you need. A few official maps for the map nerds out there:

  • Tube map (PDF) — the iconic one; every line and zone
  • Bus maps — area-by-area spider maps; buses are the scenic, cheap way to see the city from the top deck
  • Cycle map — the Cycleways network and Santander Cycles ("Boris bikes") docking stations
  • Walking maps — the "Legible London" wayfinding maps; central London is far more walkable than it looks
  • All TfL maps & the TfL Go app — river services, night tube, step-free access and live journey planning in one place

A few things to know about London

London is one of the safest major cities in the world. The violent crime rate per capita is lower than Berlin, Brussels, Barcelona, Madrid, Milan, Paris, and every single US state! The data is here, if you'd like to read it.

Normal urban common sense applies: keep an eye on your bag in crowded areas (markets, tube stations, tourist spots), keep your phone in your pocket on busy Tube platforms, and look right before crossing the road (traffic comes from the right). Nowhere you would realistically go as a tourist (Zone 1 & 2) should be too dangerous.

London has more trees per square kilometre than many cities, and is officially one of the world's greenest cities, technically classified as an "urban forest." The Royal Parks (Hyde Park, Regent's Park, Greenwich Park, Richmond Park) are enormous and entirely free. If you're visiting in late May, the city will be in full bloom.

Choose your adventure

London is enormous! You can live a lifetime and still have so much to see. We have tried to break down London into categories so that you can pick and choose how you see this amazing and crazy city.

For sightseeing

First-timers

  • The South Bank walk — Cross Westminster Bridge past Big Ben and the Houses of Parliament, follow the river east along the South Bank past the London Eye, Shakespeare's Globe, the Tate Modern, and on to Borough Market. 2–3 hours and roughly 3 miles — one of the best afternoons in the city. Free.
  • Tower of London & Tower Bridge — Crown Jewels, ravens, a millennium of intrigue at the Tower; and the iconic Victorian bascule bridge right next door. (NB: most tourists actually think Tower Bridge is London Bridge — the real London Bridge is a fairly unremarkable modern crossing nearby.) Book the Tower ahead.
  • Buckingham Palace & Changing of the Guard — Free, punctual, very shiny.
  • The Royal Parks — Hyde Park (paddle-board on the Serpentine), Regent's Park (Queen Mary's Rose Garden in bloom in late May), Green Park (quiet and beautiful, right next to the Palace), St James's Park (pelicans, the best view of Buckingham Palace, and a lake full of waterfowl), Hampstead Heath (go to Parliament Hill for the best view of the skyline).
  • Wander Chinatown — Great food and atmosphere. Try Kova Patisserie and Mamasons Dirty Ice Cream.

Shopping & Markets

London is a world-class shopping destination — from grand department stores to quirky independent markets.

  • Oxford Street & Oxford Circus — London's famous high street: Selfridges, Primark, Zara, and every major brand. One of the world's busiest shopping streets.
  • Covent Garden — The Piazza, boutique shops, street performers, and great pre-theatre buzz. Don't miss the Apple Market inside.
  • Carnaby Street & Liberty London — Liberty's iconic Tudor building and famous print fabrics, just steps from Carnaby's eclectic boutiques.
  • Harrods — London's most famous luxury department store in Knightsbridge. Worth a wander even if you're just window shopping — the food halls alone are spectacular.
  • Fortnum & Mason, Piccadilly — The world's most beautiful grocery store. Exceptional teas, biscuits, and hampers to take home.
  • Neal's Yard & Seven Dials, Covent Garden — Colourful hidden alley with independent shops and a food market above.
  • King's Road, Chelsea — West London's famous shopping strip. Boutiques, vintage, and that classic Chelsea feel.
  • Spitalfields Market & Brick Lane — East London's vintage and independent shopping heart, with bagels, street art, and Sunday market energy.
  • Columbia Road Flower Market — London's most photogenic market, open Sunday mornings only. Go early.
  • House of Mina Lima — The graphic design studio behind Harry Potter's visual world. Beautiful limited-edition prints to buy, free to browse.
London by Region

For those who've done the tourist trail and want to go deeper. This is a breakdown of the different areas of London outside your essentials. Each area can fill its own day and may have a completely different feel, history and cuisine — London is not one city but many.

🌿 North London — Megan lived here 2023–2024

  • Word on the Water — A floating bookshop moored on the Regent's Canal near Kings Cross. Tiny and charming.
  • Camden Market & Regent's Canal — Eclectic market, street food from everywhere, and a great canal-side walk.
  • Primrose Hill — The best panoramic view of the London skyline, beloved by locals. Beautiful and quiet.
  • Parkland Walk — A 5km trail through old railway cuttings from Finsbury Park to Alexandra Palace. One of London's hidden gems.
  • Blackstock Road restaurants — Megan's local strip — brilliant, affordable, and largely undiscovered by tourists.
  • Arsenal Stadium / Emirates — Stadium tours available, or catch a match if one falls during your visit.
  • Hampstead Heath & Parliament Hill view — The best view of the London skyline, a wild swimming pond, and a proper village feel at the top of the hill.
  • Highgate Village — Georgian streets, independent shops, and Highgate Cemetery (where Karl Marx and George Eliot are buried).

🏙️ East London — Kazimir lived here 2022–2024

  • Shoreditch — Street art, independent bars, vintage shops, and some of the best restaurants in the city. The heartbeat of East London.
  • Brick Lane bagels — Beigel Bake — Open 24 hours. Salt beef beigel for about £3. Legendary.
  • Brick Lane curry — The famous curry mile, where restaurants spill onto the street competing loudly for your table. Great for a group dinner — and yes, you can barter for the price of your meal.
  • Regent's Canal walk — Follow the towpath from Victoria Park or Broadway Market west through Hackney and into the city. One of London's great walks.
  • Victoria Park — East London's answer to Hyde Park, with a boating lake, great cafés, and weekend markets.
  • Jack the Ripper tour — Evening tours around Whitechapel and Spitalfields. Atmospheric and genuinely gripping.
  • Columbia Road Flower Market — Sunday mornings only. London's most photogenic market — arrive early, leave with armfuls of flowers.
  • Greenwich & Greenwich Park — Royal Observatory, the Prime Meridian, the Old Royal Naval College (free), and a brilliant market. Tip: get the Uber Boat back — a gorgeous river journey.

🌉 South London — Megan worked here 2022–2025

  • The South Bank walk — Shakespeare's Globe, Tate Modern, Borough Market, the skate park under Waterloo Bridge, the Southbank Centre. Could spend a full day without leaving.
  • Borough Market — One of the world's great food markets. Arrive hungry; leave sideways.
  • Brixton Village — Vibrant covered market halls packed with independent restaurants, bars, and shops. One of the most exciting food destinations in London.
  • Crystal Palace Park — Home to the world's first life-size dinosaur sculptures (1854), a boating lake, and sweeping views across London.
  • American Embassy, Nine Elms — The striking new US Embassy building in Nine Elms, opened 2018. For the Americans among you — if you need a hit of patriotism, here it is.
  • Battersea Power Station — The iconic Pink Floyd album cover, now a spectacular shopping and dining destination. The chimney views are unmissable.
  • Greenwich Foot Tunnel / riverside — Walk under the Thames through the Victorian tunnel and emerge in Greenwich for the Royal Observatory and park.
  • Dulwich Village & Dulwich Park — One of London's most beautiful and unexpected villages, with the Dulwich Picture Gallery (England's oldest public art gallery) and a gorgeous park.

🏡 West London — Megan lived here 2022–2023

  • Notting Hill & Portobello Road Market — Megan used to live at the top. Antiques, vintage, great food stalls (especially Saturdays), and the pastel townhouses made famous by the film.
  • Holland Park & Kyoto Garden — One of London's most beautiful and peaceful parks, with a serene Japanese garden at its heart. Peacocks included.
  • Kensington High Street — Great shopping, independent cafés, and the gateway to the museums and parks.
  • South Kensington museums — Natural History Museum and V&A within a 5-minute walk of each other. Both free, both world-class.
  • Hyde Park & Kensington Gardens — One enormous green space running from Marble Arch to the Palace. Paddle on the Serpentine, visit the Albert Memorial, find Peter Pan's statue.
  • Little Venice canal walk — Where the Grand Union and Regent's canals meet. Walk the towpath east toward Camden for a classic London afternoon.
  • Richmond riverside (if venturing further west) — Ted Lasso was filmed here. Wild deer in the park, gorgeous riverside village, and the best Sunday roast options in London.
  • Dinner in Hammersmith or Notting Hill — Both have excellent restaurant strips for an evening out.
For culture, churches & history

London has an incredibly diverse history across more than 2 millennia. To explore it properly you must visit some of London's museums. Note that the vast majority of London's museums are free!

History

  • Natural History Museum — Free. Dinosaurs, Darwin, the dazzling Hintze Hall. Wins automatically. (Kazimir's favourite museum.)
  • Science Museum — Free. Space capsules, interactive galleries, climate science. Next door to the NHM.
  • London Transport Museum — In Covent Garden. The story of how London moves, from horse-drawn buses to the Tube.
  • British Museum — Free. Rosetta Stone, Elgin Marbles, Egyptian mummies. One of the world's great collections. The Bayeux Tapestry has just moved to the British Museum — tickets for this need to be booked ASAP!
  • Victoria and Albert Museum (V&A) — Free. The world's greatest museum of art and design — fashion, furniture, jewellery, sculpture.
  • Tate Modern — Free. Modern art in a converted power station. Great café with South Bank views.
  • National Gallery — Free. Trafalgar Square's world-class collection — Da Vinci, Van Gogh, Rembrandt, Vermeer.
  • National Portrait Gallery — Free. Centuries of British history told through its most famous faces — from Tudor monarchs to contemporary icons. Recently reopened after a major refurbishment.
  • Churchill War Rooms — The underground bunker from which Churchill ran World War II. Preserved exactly as it was left. One of London's most atmospheric attractions — don't miss it.
  • London Dungeons — A theatrical, spine-tingling tour through London's darkest history. Low-key a highlight for Megan and Kazimir.
  • Evensong at St Paul's Cathedral or Westminster Abbey — Free if you attend the service. Hearing a professional choir in one of these spaces is one of the best free experiences London offers.
  • Tower of London — Crown Jewels, the Traitor's Gate, and almost 1,000 years of history. Arguably more history per square foot than anywhere in Britain.

Walking Tours

  • Harry Potter locations — Start at Leadenhall Market (the entrance to Diagon Alley), take in Australia House (Gringotts Bank) and finish near Kings Cross Station. Several guided tour operators run the full route — search "Harry Potter walking tour London."
  • Jack the Ripper tour — The original unsolved mystery. Evening tours around Whitechapel and Spitalfields are atmospheric, well-researched and genuinely gripping. One of London's most popular walking tours.
  • London history walking tours — The Original London Walks runs guided tours covering Roman London, medieval streets, the Great Fire, and much more. Excellent value and led by expert guides.

Theatre, music & comedy

  • West End shows — Buy tickets via the show's own website, or monitor TodayTix for last-minute deals. Our picks for May/June 2027:
    • The Lion King — Lyceum Theatre. The staging and costumes are unlike anything else in the West End; a genuine spectacle.
    • Wicked — Apollo Victoria Theatre. If you saw the film, see it live — the stage version is on another level.
    • Hamilton — Victoria Palace Theatre. The one everyone quotes; still excellent years on.
    • Les Misérables — Sondheim Theatre. The definitive production of the world's most beloved musical.
    • The Phantom of the Opera — His Majesty's Theatre. A West End institution.
    • Six — Vaudeville Theatre. Henry VIII's six wives as a pop concert. Brilliantly done and enormous fun.
    • The Book of Mormon — Prince of Wales Theatre. Not for the easily offended.
    • The Mousetrap — St Martin's Theatre. Agatha Christie's whodunit, in continuous performance since 1952 — the longest-running show in West End history.
  • Royal Opera House / Royal Ballet — Classics in the most beautiful opera house in the UK. Cheap nosebleed seats exist if you book early.
  • Sadler's Wells — Contemporary dance at its best. More accessible prices and a more adventurous programme than the ROH.
  • Top Secret Comedy Club — Monday night is when artists are trying comedy for the first time, but you can see incredible acts for an insane £1. A favourite of Grace's.
  • Shakespeare's Globe — Outdoor performances in the reconstructed Globe Theatre on the South Bank. Standing "groundling" tickets are just £5.

Cinema

  • Everyman Cinema — London's most beloved independent cinema chain: sofas, waiter service, and a proper bar. Venues across the city.
  • Picturehouse Cinemas — Independent cinemas with a great atmosphere and a strong programme of mainstream and arthouse films.
  • BFI Southbank — The British Film Institute's flagship cinema on the South Bank. Classics, retrospectives, and special screenings in stunning surroundings.

Churches

London is packed with beautiful churches tucked between streets — don't just walk past them. Pop into as many as you find; most are free, almost all are stunning, and they're often the quietest spots in the city.

  • St Martin-in-the-Fields — Megan & Kazimir's London church, with a world-class musical programme. Free lunchtime concerts most Mondays, Tuesdays, and Fridays — one of the best free experiences in London.
  • St Paul's Cathedral — Wren's masterpiece, dome views, Whispering Gallery and the crypt where Nelson and Wellington are buried. Note: entry charges apply for sightseeing — we'd recommend going to Evensong instead (free, and one of the most beautiful experiences London offers).
  • Westminster Abbey — The coronation church; burial place of monarchs, poets and scientists. Note: entry charges apply — again, Evensong is free and utterly magical. Hearing a professional choir in either of these spaces is one of the best things you can do in London.
For food & a great night out

London's food scene is among the best in the world. These are our honest picks — places we've been to repeatedly or sent friends to and heard back from.

Restaurants

  • Dishoom — Incredible Indian food at any of their London locations. There's always a queue; it's always worth it. The bacon naan roll for breakfast is legendary.
  • Kingly Court — Imad's Syrian Kitchen (the best Syrian food you'll have outside Damascus) and La Maritxu Basque Cheesecake.
  • Ngon Ngon or What The Pok'k — Amazing Vietnamese near Farringdon (direct train from St Albans).
  • Fish, Wings and Tings, Brixton — Really good Caribbean and one of Megan's favourite restaurants in London.
  • Any of the Big Mamma Italian group restaurants — spectacular rooms and excellent food.
  • The Cheese Barge, Paddington — Fresh food and the most amazing cheese on a canal boat. Exactly what it sounds like.
  • Norfolk Arms, Kings Cross — A beautiful pub that randomly serves superb Mediterranean tapas.
  • Borough Market — Arrive hungry, leave sideways. Go for lunch on a weekday to avoid peak weekend crowds.
  • Xi Home Dumplings Bay near Leicester Square — Some of the best Chinese dumplings in London.

Historic Pubs

London has some of the oldest and most atmospheric pubs in the world — many with centuries of history, literary connections, and extraordinary interiors. If you want to pick a few to try, this list of London's top historic pubs is a great starting point.

A night to remember — date night ideas

  • Sky Garden at golden hour — the best free view in London. Book a 6pm slot and watch the sun set over the city. One of the most romantic things you can do for £0.
  • A riverside walk from St Paul's to the Tower of London, followed by dinner in Shoreditch — the classic London date-night route.
  • Jazz at Ronnie Scott's — London's legendary jazz club in Soho. Book ahead; world-class musicians every night.
  • Cahoots Underground — Cocktails in a 1940s London Underground car. One of the most fun bars in the city.
  • Gordon's Wine Bar — Candlelit, atmospheric, right by the Thames. London's oldest wine bar — been there since 1890.
  • An evening at the Globe or a West End show — followed by dinner on the South Bank or in the West End.

Coffee & sweet treats

  • Coffee — Megan's picks: The Fleet Street Press, Kiss the Hippo Coffee (Covent Garden), Project68 (Kings Cross), Chealsons Coffee (near Tate Modern), Lever & Bloom (near Tottenham Court Road).
  • In Chinatown: Kova Patisserie — Megan's go-to dessert spot; try the sea-salt cheesecake. Also Mamasons Dirty Ice Cream for ube bilog (purple ice cream in a crepe).
  • Udderlicious Ice Cream — Amazing in Covent Garden and Islington.
  • Humble Crumble — A must. Crumble is a classic British dessert and this is the definitive version.
  • Voila! Wine Bar near the West End — A lovely spot for a glass of wine after a show.
  • Afternoon tea — You're in England; it would be rude not to. Try the Wolseley, Fortnum & Mason, or push the boat out at a grand hotel like Claridge's.
